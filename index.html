About Me

I studied mechanical engineering through a joint undergraduate program at Chongqing University and the University of Cincinnati. I earned my M.Eng. at UC Berkeley, where I worked with Mark Mueller.

Research Interests

My research goal is to build trustworthy AI. I study how training shapes the reliability of a model’s reasoning, how an agent’s conclusions can be checked against evidence, and when a robot can apply a learned skill beyond its training examples.

  • Reliable reasoning in language models. In CRF, I study how to help a smaller model recover when its reasoning drifts from its teacher’s. In GRT, I examine why verifier-based post-training can narrow the range of correct solutions a model produces and how to preserve that diversity.
  • Interpretability and verification. Perception Chains links each visual reasoning step to an image region and an observed attribute, so a mistaken inference can be traced to its evidence. In ProvableRAG, we use symbolic proof checking to determine which facts would suffice to answer a question and whether the retrieved premises support the conclusion.
  • Generalization and planning in embodied AI. My robot learning research tests whether a policy can apply a familiar skill to a familiar object when that pairing was absent from fine-tuning. In RADI, I study how an embodied agent can use predicted action effects to identify an infeasible step and revise its plan in simulation.
