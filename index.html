About Me

I studied mechanical engineering through a joint undergraduate program at Chongqing University and the University of Cincinnati. I earned my M.Eng. at UC Berkeley, where I worked with Mark Mueller.

Research Interests

  • Natural Language Processing. I study how language models learn to reason during post-training. My work examines how reasoning transfers to smaller models, how training affects the diversity of correct solutions, and whether changes in a model’s internal representations can guide curriculum learning. For language agents, I use symbolic proof checking to plan multi-hop retrieval and verify whether the retrieved evidence supports an answer.
  • Multimodal Learning. My interests are visual grounding, spatial reasoning, and the interpretability of vision-language models. I study how to link individual reasoning steps to image regions and their attributes, and how to use errors in predicted spatial relations to supervise representation learning.
  • Robot Learning. I study generalization in robot manipulation and task planning for embodied agents. I examine whether a vision-language-action policy can perform familiar skills on familiar objects in combinations absent from its fine-tuning data. My work on planning uses world models to predict action effects and revise plans for household tasks.
